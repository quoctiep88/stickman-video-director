Write as one person explaining something to another. Read the Series bible and narration direction, episode brief, plan and relevant visual beats. For Computing Clearly, use the [Series bible supplement](../../docs/qtx/narration/computing-clearly.md). Keep experiments outside the approved episode.

Draft from the listener's immediate experience. Keep track of who does what, what is merely possible, and which facts the listener needs to understand the next beat. Use [spoken writing](references/spoken-writing.md) for phrasing and density, [pattern audit](references/anti-ai-patterns.md) for habitual wording, and [examples](references/examples.md) when a revision feels forced.

## Read-aloud pass

Before presenting a draft for approval, read each beat at conversational pace. Where would you breathe? Does the punctuation put the pause there? Listen for the word that gets the stress and whether it carries the idea. Would someone actually say this sentence? If several lines land on the same cadence, vary the phrasing. Read the last line into the next beat. Does the speaker have a reason to go there? Listen for accidental grammatical echoes such as "asking X ... asking Y." Name the actors when an abstract phrase hides who asks, who acts or what comes back. Rewrite the stumble, read it again, then check the facts against the source.

Prepare the exact words for approval using the [delivery guidance](references/elevenlabs-delivery.md). A writing review authorizes no generation.

## Final check

- FACTS and CONCRETENESS — verify actors, sequence and qualifiers against the brief. Can the listener picture the example?
- SPOKEN RHYTHM and TRANSITIONS — breathe at the intended pauses. Follow the thought across each cut.
- AI PATTERNS and REDUNDANCY — cut automatic hooks, echoes and recaps. Give the visual time to work.
- SERIES VOICE and TTS READABILITY — hear the intended speaker in the wording. Check that every word sent to voice belongs in the approved script.
