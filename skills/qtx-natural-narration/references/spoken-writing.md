# Spoken writing

Start with an event the listener can picture. Let an observation raise a real question, explain what happened, then name the concept when the name helps. Follow a consequence or surprise into the next beat. A definition, example and recap at every turn soon feels like a lesson being recited.

Give each breath one idea. Split a sentence where a speaker would naturally pause. Let a short line put weight on a detail, then let a longer sentence carry a connected thought. Use contractions such as “doesn't” and “you're” when natural. Keep the full form for deliberate emphasis. An occasional fragment can land a point. A whole paragraph of fragments can sound like a checklist.

Read alongside the visual plan. If arrows already show the route, spend the line on why the request goes there. Leave a beat for the viewer to notice the result. If a passage feels empty, remove the paraphrase or filler and move to the next useful idea. Preserve facts even when a simpler claim would scan better.
